Case Study Spotify 8/10

How Spotify nails first-run personalization

Spotify turns a cold start into a tailored experience before you've made a single deliberate choice — here's the machinery behind it.

By Editorial Team · 8 June 2026 · ≈ 1 min read

A music app with no music is the hardest empty state there is. Spotify solves it by turning sign-up into a lightweight taste survey — so the home screen is already yours the first time you see it.

Make the cold start feel warm

Recommendation engines need a signal. Rather than guess, Spotify asks for three to five artists up front. It feels like fun, not data entry — but it’s enough to seed every playlist that follows.

1

Trade a tiny task for a huge payoff

A 20-second taste picker buys you a personalized home screen. Users happily pay that price because the reward is immediate and visible.

Show the payoff instantly

The moment onboarding ends, the home screen is full of recommendations tied to the artists you chose. There’s no empty library, no “add your first song” dead end.

Keep learning quietly

Every play, skip and save refines the model in the background. The user never manages settings — the product just gets more accurate, which keeps them coming back.

2

Let behaviour be the configuration

Implicit signals (plays, skips) beat explicit ones (forms, toggles). Design the product to learn from what people do, not what they declare.

The takeaway

Spotify treats personalization as onboarding, not a setting. A tiny upfront ask plus relentless background learning means the product feels bespoke from minute one.
