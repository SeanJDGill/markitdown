A music app with no music is the hardest empty state there is. Spotify solves it by
turning sign-up into a lightweight taste survey — so the home screen is already *yours*
the first time you see it.

## Make the cold start feel warm

Recommendation engines need a signal. Rather than guess, Spotify asks for three to five
artists up front. It feels like fun, not data entry — but it's enough to seed every
playlist that follows.

<Insight number={1} title="Trade a tiny task for a huge payoff">
  A 20-second taste picker buys you a personalized home screen. Users happily pay that
  price because the reward is immediate and visible.
</Insight>

## Show the payoff instantly

The moment onboarding ends, the home screen is full of recommendations tied to the artists
you chose. There's no empty library, no "add your first song" dead end.

<PullQuote cite="Spotify onboarding">
  The best cure for an empty state is to never let the user see one.
</PullQuote>

## Keep learning quietly

Every play, skip and save refines the model in the background. The user never manages
settings — the product just gets more accurate, which keeps them coming back.

<Insight number={2} title="Let behaviour be the configuration">
  Implicit signals (plays, skips) beat explicit ones (forms, toggles). Design the product
  to learn from what people do, not what they declare.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Spotify treats personalization as onboarding, not a setting. A tiny upfront ask plus
relentless background learning means the product feels bespoke from minute one.
