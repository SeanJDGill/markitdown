GOV.UK serves everyone in the country — including people on old phones, slow connections,
and assistive technology, often dealing with stressful, high-stakes tasks. Its design
system is a masterclass in what's left when you strip away everything that isn't service.

## One thing per page

GOV.UK's signature pattern is asking just one question per screen. It looks slow; it's
faster. Each page is trivial to understand, easy to validate, and painless to resume after
an interruption.

<Insight number={1} title="One question per page beats one long form">
  Splitting a form into single questions reduces cognitive load, simplifies error handling,
  and makes the whole flow resilient to interruptions.
</Insight>

## Plain language, generous targets

Labels are written at a reading age the whole population can follow. Touch targets are
large, contrast is high, and the layout reflows cleanly to any screen. None of this is
decoration — it's the difference between a service working and not.

<PullQuote cite="GOV.UK design principles">
  This is for everyone. If it doesn't work for the person in the hardest situation, it
  doesn't work.
</PullQuote>

## Errors that help, not scold

When something's wrong, GOV.UK lists every error at the top with anchor links, repeats the
message beside each field, and never clears the user's answers. The system assumes mistakes
are the design's fault, not the user's.

<Insight number={2} title="Treat errors as a design failure">
  Summarise errors, link to each field, keep the user's input, and explain the fix. Blame
  the form, never the person.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

GOV.UK proves accessibility and simplicity aren't constraints on good design — they *are*
good design. One question per page, plain words and humane errors make a service that
genuinely works for everyone.
