Most sign-ups ask for your money before they've earned your trust. Netflix flips the
order: it sells the catalogue, lets you self-select a plan, and only then asks for
payment — by which point you're already imagining tonight's binge.

## Start with the smallest commitment

The landing page asks for an email and nothing else. It's a near-zero-cost first step
that gets the user moving — and once people start, they tend to finish.

<Insight number={1} title="Lower the cost of step one">
  The first action should be almost free. Momentum, not information, is what you're
  collecting at the top of the funnel.
</Insight>

## Let people choose how much to commit

The plan picker presents tiers side by side with clear differences. By choosing a plan
themselves, users feel in control rather than upsold.

<Screenshot
  src="/placeholders/screen-b.svg"
  alt="Netflix plan comparison"
  caption="Three tiers, plain differences — the user does the qualifying."
  number={2}
/>

<PullQuote>
  Ask for commitment in increasing increments. Email, then plan, then payment — never all
  at once.
</PullQuote>

## Reassure at the scary step

At payment, Netflix surfaces "cancel anytime" and the exact billing date. Naming the risk
out loud is what makes the final click feel safe.

<Insight number={2} title="Defuse the last objection at the last step">
  Put your strongest reassurance exactly where doubt peaks — the payment screen.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Netflix sequences commitment like a story: each step is small, self-chosen, and
reassuring, so the payment screen feels like a formality rather than a decision.
