Choosing where to stay is a high-stakes, low-information decision: you're committing real
money to a place you've never been. Airbnb's search is engineered to convert that anxiety
into confidence, step by step.

## Answer the question behind the search

The real question isn't "what's available" — it's "will this be good, and where exactly is
it?" The map-plus-list view answers location instantly, and photos answer quality before a
single word is read.

<Insight number={1} title="Design for the decision, not the query">
  Users don't want search results; they want to feel confident choosing. Surface the
  information that actually drives the decision.
</Insight>

## Keep feedback live

As you drag filters or pan the map, results and counts update immediately. You never apply
a filter and discover it returned nothing — the system shows the consequence before you
commit to it.

<PullQuote cite="Airbnb">
  Every filter should show its effect before the user has to trust it.
</PullQuote>

## Build trust before the booking

Reviews, superhost badges, and clear cancellation terms sit right where hesitation peaks.
By the time you reach the booking button, the doubts have been answered in order.

<Screenshot
  src="/placeholders/screen-b.svg"

<Insight number={2} title="Place reassurance where doubt lives">
  Map your users' hesitations to specific moments, and answer each one right there — not in
  a FAQ they'll never open.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Airbnb's search succeeds by treating browsing as a confidence funnel: show location and
quality first, keep feedback live, and answer each doubt exactly where it arises.
