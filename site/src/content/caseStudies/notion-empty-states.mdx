Notion's power is also its problem: it can be *anything*, which means a new user faces the
most intimidating thing in software — a blank page. Notion's answer is to never leave that
page truly blank.

## Replace the blank canvas with a prompt

A new page shows ghost text and a slash-command hint. The user is never staring at
nothing — there's always a suggested next keystroke.

<Insight number={1} title="An empty state is a question, not a void">
  Every blank surface should answer "what do I do here?" before the user has to ask.
</Insight>

## Teach the system through templates

Pre-built templates aren't just shortcuts — they're worked examples. Opening one shows the
user how databases, toggles and links fit together, far better than any tutorial.

<Screenshot
  src="/placeholders/screen-b.svg"
  alt="Notion template gallery"
  caption="Templates double as documentation you learn by editing."
  number={2}
/>

<PullQuote cite="Notion">
  Show people a finished example they can take apart — it beats a tour they'll click
  through and forget.
</PullQuote>

## Progressive complexity

Notion hides its depth behind the slash menu, so beginners see a simple text editor while
power users summon databases on demand. The interface grows with the user.

<Insight number={2} title="Hide power until it's wanted">
  Default to simple. Let complexity be something the user reaches for, not something that
  greets them.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Notion tames an infinitely flexible product by making its empty states do the teaching —
prompts, templates and progressive disclosure turn "what now?" into "oh, I get it."
