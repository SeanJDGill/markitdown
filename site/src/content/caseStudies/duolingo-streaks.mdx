A streak is just a number that counts consecutive days. On paper it's trivial. In
practice it's one of the most effective retention mechanics ever shipped — and Duolingo
has spent years refining exactly how it feels to grow one, and to lose one.

## Loss aversion is the engine

People work harder to avoid losing something than to gain something of equal value. Once
your streak hits double digits, the asset you're protecting feels real — and skipping a
day becomes a loss, not a non-event.

<Insight number={1} title="Make the thing losable">
  A streak only motivates because it can break. The fragility is the feature.
</Insight>

## But never punish too hard

A purely punitive streak would burn people out. Duolingo softens the edges with **streak
freezes** and **repair** — mechanics that let a committed user recover from a slip without
losing months of progress.

  src="/placeholders/screen-b.svg"

<PullQuote>
  The goal isn't to make people feel bad for missing a day — it's to make them feel proud
  of every day they didn't.
</PullQuote>

## Nudge at the right moment

The famous reminder notifications work because they're *timed* and *escalating*, not just
frequent. A nudge at your usual practice time respects an existing habit instead of
inventing a new interruption.

<Insight number={2} title="Reinforce habits, don't manufacture them">
  Notifications land best when they align with a routine the user already has.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Duolingo's streak is a masterclass in balancing pressure and mercy: enough loss aversion
to motivate, enough forgiveness to retain.
