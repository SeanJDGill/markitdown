Case Study Netflix 8.5/10

Netflix's sign-up is a masterclass in commitment

Netflix reorders the classic funnel — show the value, then ask for the plan, then the payment — and removes every reason to bail.

By Editorial Team · 9 June 2026 · ≈ 1 min read

Most sign-ups ask for your money before they’ve earned your trust. Netflix flips the order: it sells the catalogue, lets you self-select a plan, and only then asks for payment — by which point you’re already imagining tonight’s binge.

Start with the smallest commitment

The landing page asks for an email and nothing else. It’s a near-zero-cost first step that gets the user moving — and once people start, they tend to finish.

1

Lower the cost of step one

The first action should be almost free. Momentum, not information, is what you’re collecting at the top of the funnel.

Let people choose how much to commit

The plan picker presents tiers side by side with clear differences. By choosing a plan themselves, users feel in control rather than upsold.

Reassure at the scary step

At payment, Netflix surfaces “cancel anytime” and the exact billing date. Naming the risk out loud is what makes the final click feel safe.

2

Defuse the last objection at the last step

Put your strongest reassurance exactly where doubt peaks — the payment screen.

The takeaway

Netflix sequences commitment like a story: each step is small, self-chosen, and reassuring, so the payment screen feels like a formality rather than a decision.
