Meditation has no external deadline and no obvious reward — the perfect recipe for a habit
that never forms. Headspace engineers the missing structure: small starts, gentle
reminders and progress you can see.

## Shrink the first step

The opening session is just a few minutes. By making the entry cost trivially low,
Headspace removes the main excuse — "I don't have time" — before it can form.

<Insight number={1} title="Make starting easier than skipping">
  A three-minute first action lowers the activation energy so far that doing it is easier
  than rationalising not doing it.
</Insight>

## Anchor the habit to a time

During onboarding, Headspace asks *when* you want to meditate and sets a reminder for that
slot. Tying the new behaviour to a chosen time turns intention into a scheduled cue.

<PullQuote cite="Headspace">
  Habits don't form from motivation — they form from cues. Pick the cue with the user.
</PullQuote>

## Make progress visible and forgiving

Run totals and gentle streaks reward consistency, but the tone stays kind: miss a day and
the app encourages rather than scolds — protecting the relationship, not just the metric.

  src="/placeholders/screen-b.svg"

<Insight number={2} title="Reward consistency, forgive lapses">
  Visible progress motivates; a forgiving tone retains. You need both, or burnout wins.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Headspace manufactures the structure meditation lacks — tiny first steps, a chosen cue,
and kind, visible progress — turning a vague intention into a daily habit.
