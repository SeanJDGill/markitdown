Opening a bank account used to mean a branch visit, two forms of ID and a week of
waiting. Monzo compresses the whole thing into a few minutes on your phone — and it
does so by being ruthless about *what it asks, and when*.

## Ask for less, sooner

The fastest way to lose someone in sign-up is to show them a wall of fields. Monzo
front-loads almost nothing: it asks for an email, then immediately gets you *doing*
something rather than *filling something in*.

<Insight number={1} title="Defer everything you can">
  Every field you can collect later is a field that isn't standing between the user and
  their first win. Monzo collects legal identity data only once you're invested enough to
  want the account.
</Insight>

## Borrow trust you haven't earned yet

A new fintech has no brand equity. Monzo leans on cues the user already trusts: the
phone's native camera for ID capture, Face ID for the photo step, and copy that names
the regulator explicitly.

  The best onboarding doesn't feel like onboarding. It feels like you've already started.

## Turn the dead time into a story

Account checks take real seconds. Instead of a spinner, Monzo narrates progress —
"Creating your account", "Setting things up" — which reframes waiting as work being done
*for* you.

<Insight number={2} title="Narrate the wait">
  A labelled progress sequence feels faster than an identical spinner, because attention is
  on the story, not the clock.
</Insight>

## The takeaway

Monzo's sign-up wins not through clever screens but through restraint: ask for less,
sequence it carefully, and borrow trust at the exact moment doubt creeps in.
