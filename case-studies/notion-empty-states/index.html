Case Study Notion 7.5/10

Notion's empty states teach you the product

A blank page is terrifying. Notion turns every empty surface into a guided, fill-in-the-blank lesson in how the tool works.

By Editorial Team · 10 June 2026 · ≈ 1 min read

Notion’s power is also its problem: it can be anything, which means a new user faces the most intimidating thing in software — a blank page. Notion’s answer is to never leave that page truly blank.

Replace the blank canvas with a prompt

A new page shows ghost text and a slash-command hint. The user is never staring at nothing — there’s always a suggested next keystroke.

1

An empty state is a question, not a void

Every blank surface should answer “what do I do here?” before the user has to ask.

Teach the system through templates

Pre-built templates aren’t just shortcuts — they’re worked examples. Opening one shows the user how databases, toggles and links fit together, far better than any tutorial.

Progressive complexity

Notion hides its depth behind the slash menu, so beginners see a simple text editor while power users summon databases on demand. The interface grows with the user.

2

Hide power until it's wanted

Default to simple. Let complexity be something the user reaches for, not something that greets them.

The takeaway

Notion tames an infinitely flexible product by making its empty states do the teaching — prompts, templates and progressive disclosure turn “what now?” into “oh, I get it.”
